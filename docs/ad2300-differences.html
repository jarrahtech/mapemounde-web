2300 AD: differences from the book

This page assumes you already know the book. It lists only where the generator departs from it.

Book
2300 AD: Man’s Battle for the Stars, Marc W. Miller, Timothy B. Brown, Lester W. Smith and Frank Chadwick, Game Designers’ Workshop, 1988. No version or printing number is given; the copy used includes the errata sheet of 19 October 1988.
Rules implemented
The World Generation chapter of the Director’s Guide
Compared against
mapemounde library 0.5.0, as at 8 October 2026

Stars

  • Stars are generated randomly: the number of stars, their spectral type and size, and the separation of companions. None of this is in the book, which has the referee choose a star from the Near Star List.

Orbits

  • Empty orbits are never rolled, so generation does not stop after two consecutive empty orbits. Instead about 10% of orbits are removed at random and one in six of those becomes an asteroid belt.
  • That removal happens after orbits are checked for stability and vaporisation, not before.
  • An orbit is vaporised if its blackbody temperature is 2,000 K or more, calculated directly, not read from the Untenable Orbits table.
  • The vaporisation and inside-the-star checks count every star in the system, not only the star being orbited.
  • An orbit beyond three times the distance to a companion is stable only if the companion is lighter than the star being orbited.
  • Around O, B and A stars, chunks that roll the same orbital radius are merged into one.

Worlds

  • Mass and surface gravity are calculated from the world’s diameter and density, not read from the World Mass and Gravity tables.
  • Atmospheric pressure equals surface gravity in Gs (the condensed table).
  • A world that snowballs is given a new Icy density reduced by 10%. The book leaves its density unchanged.
  • Where the Average Temperatures table gives a range, a single class is used (Cold–Hot becomes Hot).
  • Ocean and ice sheet coverage is 2D6 × 10%, capped at 100%, so it is never below 20%. The book uses (2D6 − 2) × 10%.
  • The atmosphere type the book calls “Standard” is named “Moderate”.

Satellites

  • A satellite’s radius is capped at 0.8 of its world’s radius, in place of the book’s repeated halving. The cap also applies to chunk satellites.
  • A satellite over 200 km in diameter that rolls an orbit below 3 is placed at 3. The book rerolls.
  • A satellite with a size roll below 0 is always a ring, placed in a close orbit.
  • For satellites whose size falls between or outside the rows of the Minimum Molecular Weight Retained table, the nearest row and column are used, a “vac” entry is read as 120, and a world under 500 km in diameter is a vacuum.

Not implemented

  • Colony and social data.
